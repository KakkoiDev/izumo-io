The Workbench

Build real, maintainable apps from zero to production with Pi Workbench, a local AI coding workbench. No coding or AI experience needed.

Part 1: Working with Pi

What the workbench is, how to ask, and how to read what Pi did

Meeting your new apprentice before you hand over the keys

W01

What the Workbench Is (and the Three AI Risks)

Pi Workbench is a local workshop where an AI agent builds your app and a checker watches every move. What it does, what it does not do, and the three rules that keep you safe.

 W02

Asking Well: Requests, Context, Pi's Memory

A request is a work order. Say what you want and how you will know it works, point Pi at the right files, and keep standing instructions in AGENTS.md.

 W03

Reading What Pi Did

The foreman's notebook: the task file, a test that fails then passes, what 'verified' means, the commit the harness makes, and how to go back.

Part 2: Your First App

The server stack, one piece at a time: pages, looks, htmx, data, rules, two languages

Learning each room of the house you are building

W04

Your First App from the Starter

Pi never starts from nothing: the starter is a small, working notes app with tests. Make your copy, run it, and learn what each folder is for.

 W05

Pages: HTML in Templates

A template is a form letter with blanks. Jinja fills the blanks, base.html is the shared frame, pages are whole pages and fragments are pieces. Add your first page.

 W06

Looking Good: Basecoat and Tailwind

Furniture and floor plan: Basecoat gives ready-made components as class names, Tailwind arranges them. The classes that are almost always right, and the build step behind them.

 W07

HTML over the Wire: htmx

The kitchen sends finished plates: the server returns HTML fragments and htmx 4 puts them in place. Read the starter's add, delete and error flows, and the htmx 4 rules Pi must follow.

 W08

Data That Stays: SQLite and Migrations

One file holds all your app's data. Migrations are the numbered renovation plans that change its shape safely, and db.py is the only door in.

 W09

Rules as Small Typed, Tested Functions

Keep the app's rules in pure functions: data in, data out, no surprises. Types let pyright catch mistakes before anything runs, and small tests check every rule completely.

 W10

Two Languages: English and Japanese

Every word on the page goes through t("key"), with one dictionary per language. How the language is chosen, how to add a word, and why the starter's test keeps both dictionaries in step.

Part 3: Sharing It

History, GitHub, the phone, apps with no server, and putting it online

Opening the door so other people can come in

W11

Git, the Time Machine the Harness Drives

Git records every verified change as a snapshot. Set it up once, read the history, see exactly what changed, go back, and try ideas on a branch.

 W12

GitHub: Account, Connect, First Repository

Put a copy of your project's history on GitHub: a free account, your computer connected with gh, one command to publish, and the push you do after Pi's commits.

 W13

On the Phone: an Installable App (PWA)

Your app is already a PWA: a manifest, a service worker, an icon. Open it on your phone over HTTPS, add it to the home screen, and know what works offline.

 W14

An App with No Server at All

One question decides whether your app needs a server. If not, it runs entirely in the browser: free on GitHub Pages, works offline, nothing to keep on. What you gain, what you lose, and how people can still share live.

 W15

Native Phone Apps without a Store

When a PWA is not enough: Capacitor wraps the same app in a native shell. Installing it on Android (an APK, GitHub releases, Google's new verification) and on iPhone (what Apple allows, stated plainly).

 W16

Putting It Online

Three ways, one for each kind of app: GitHub Pages for apps with no server, Tailscale Funnel as the free default for server apps, Fly.io when it must be always on.

Part 4: Growing It

Accounts, AI inside the app, finding bugs, and shipping for real

The house stands. Now you live in it, fix it, and add rooms

W17

Logins and Accounts

Who are you, and what may you do? When an app needs accounts, the safest login you already have, and the rules for building one: hashed passwords, sessions, CSRF, HTTPS.

 W18

An App That Uses AI

Your computer already runs a model for Pi. Your app can ask it too: one typed function in the shell, pure logic around it, a test that never calls the real model, and the AI risks, now for your users.

 W19

Finding Bugs with Pi: Reproduce First

A doctor examines before prescribing. Report a bug with its conditions, have Pi see it fail before fixing it, and let a failing test prove the cause. A true story from the harness itself.

 W20

Shipping, and What Comes Next

Finishing is a skill: a shipping checklist for your app, keeping it simple so it still runs in two years, and where to go from here.
