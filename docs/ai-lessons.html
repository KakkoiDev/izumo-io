AI Lessons

A beginner course on using an AI coding assistant safely and well. No coding experience needed.

Part 1: Talking to the Machine (Using AI)

Install an assistant, learn to ask well, and never trust it blindly

Learning to use a power tool before you build furniture with it

A01

AI Risks + Set Up Your Environment

The three risks that keep you safe (with the full story one click away), then a complete WSL setup with troubleshooting.

 A02

Terminal Cheat Sheet + Install Antigravity

A pocket reference for the terminal, then install the Antigravity CLI, log in free with Google, and have your first conversation.

 A03

How to Ask: Prompting

The quality of the answer depends on the quality of the question. Be specific, give examples and constraints, and treat the first answer as a draft.

 A04

Context Engineering

The AI only knows what you put in front of it. Feed it the actual file with @, keep the context focused, and stop making it guess.

 A05

Memories with AGENTS.md

Stop re-explaining yourself every session. An AGENTS.md file gives the assistant standing instructions it reads automatically every time.

 A06

Agent Skills

Save your best prompts as reusable skills the agent reaches for on its own. Other tools call these custom commands; Antigravity calls them skills.

 A07

Making & Running Scripts with AI

A script turns a repeated chore into one command. Have the AI write scripts for you, and call the AI from inside a script. Read before you run.

 A08

Capstone: Your AI Toolkit

Put it all together. Assemble a personal AI toolkit and prove the one skill that matters most: catching the AI when it is wrong.

Part 2: Let's Make an Online Multiplayer Video Game

One real project, start to finish: a game with no server, that you can send to a friend

You have the tools. Now build the furniture, and let people sit on it

A09

Get Your Tools

Set up the three windows you will use for the whole game: the assistant that writes code, the editor that shows it, and your page in the browser.

 A10

Create the Player

A square you can drive with the keyboard or your finger — and the way of cutting a feature into blocks that you will use for the whole rest of the game.

 A11

Save the Game

Close the tab, open it again, and your square is exactly where you left it, still called by your name.

 A12

Other People

A second square on your screen, driven by another person on another computer, with no server in the middle.

 A13

Talking, Safely

Tap a phrase and it appears above your square on everyone else's screen — with a list of what can be said, and a check that drops anything else.

 A14

Your Monster

The square becomes a monster, and its legs move when it walks.

 A15

Walls

Walk into a rock and stop — and slide along it instead of sticking.

 A16

The Map

A world far bigger than the screen, that slides past you as you walk.

 A17

Sound

A footstep every time you walk, music when you ask for it, and a button that makes it all stop.

 A18

Put It Online

Give your game a real address on the internet, and get a proper undo button while you are at it.

 A19

Challenge Someone

Walk up to another player, press one key, and a fight screen opens on both computers at the same moment.

 A20

Three Moves

Pick fire, water or earth, and see on the screen who won the round and why.

 A21

Someone to Fight

A computer opponent, so there is always someone to fight even when nobody else is online.

 A22

No Peeking

Both players lock in their move before either one is shown, and a cheat who swaps their move gets caught on screen.

 A23

What We Didn't Build

The things this game deliberately does not have, what each one would have cost, and why leaving them out was the design.
