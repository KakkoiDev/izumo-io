T47: Your Own Domain Name

Your site already has an address, like your-name.github.io. It works, but it is like living at "Building 4, Unit 2187". A domain name is a street address you choose, like aiko-bakes.com, and it stays yours even if you move house. DNS is the city's address book: when someone types your name, DNS looks up which computer lives there. This lesson buys a name and writes your line in that book.

Checked 2026-10-15; prices and screens change.

Registrar vs Host

  • The registrar rents you the name. We use Namecheap (namecheap.com).
  • The host runs your site: GitHub Pages for files (A18), or a server from T44, such as Fly.io.

Your site never moves to Namecheap; only its address line does. T40 shows kakkoi.dev, a GitHub Pages site on its own domain.

You rent a name one year at a time, and there are two prices: the first-year price (often a discount) and the renewal price for every year after. A .com can cost a few dollars the first year and around 15 dollars a year later. Judge by the renewal.

Choosing a Name

  • Short and easy to say aloud. If you must spell it on the phone, it is too long.
  • No hyphens or numbers. People forget them.
  • .com if you can: people type it by habit. .dev and .app suit tech projects.
  • Check it is free by searching on Namecheap. Skip names offered for sale by their owner: they are expensive.

Registering on Namecheap

  1. On the home page, type the name into the domain search and click Search.
  2. Next to an available name, click Add to Cart, then open the cart and click Checkout.
  3. Before you click Confirm Order, read the cart:
    • One year is enough to start. Note the renewal price.
    • Auto-renewal: switch it on.
    • Domain Privacy: keep it on. Every domain has a public owner record (WHOIS); privacy hides your name, address and phone. Namecheap includes it free.
    • Skip the extras (hosting, email, PremiumDNS).
  4. Create your account: a strong password from a password manager, and an email you will still read in five years.
  5. Enter real contact details (false ones can get the name suspended; privacy hides them), choose how to pay, and click Pay Now.

Then turn on two-factor authentication, as for GitHub in T41: Profile >> Security >> Access >> Two-Factor Authentication, Enable, link your authenticator app, and store the backup codes away from your phone.

Pointing It at Your Site

DNS lives in Domain List (left sidebar), Manage next to your domain, the Advanced DNS tab. Under Host records, Add New Record adds a line. @ means the bare name; www means www.aiko-bakes.com.

First, delete old records on @ and www, such as a parking page or a redirect.

GitHub Pages

  1. In your repository: Settings → Pages. Under Custom domain, type aiko-bakes.com and click Save. Do this before DNS, so nobody else can claim your name on GitHub.
  2. In Namecheap's Advanced DNS, add these records, then click Save All Changes:
Type Host Value
A Record @ 185.199.108.153
A Record @ 185.199.109.153
A Record @ 185.199.110.153
A Record @ 185.199.111.153
CNAME Record www your-username.github.io

The CNAME never includes the repository name. GitHub's custom domain page also lists four optional AAAA records (IPv6).

Last, when the check passes, tick Enforce HTTPS on the same screen. It can take up to a day to appear.

Fly.io

For an always-on server app (W16):

fly certs add aiko-bakes.com
fly certs add www.aiko-bakes.com

Fly prints the records: A and AAAA for the bare name, a CNAME to your app's .fly.dev name for www. Copy them exactly. fly certs setup aiko-bakes.com shows them again; fly certs check aiko-bakes.com says when HTTPS is ready.

Tailscale Funnel

Funnel can only use .ts.net names. You cannot put your own domain on a Funnel; use Pages or Fly.io.

Waiting and Checking

Namecheap says new records usually work within 30 minutes; GitHub allows up to 24 hours. Check from a terminal:

dig aiko-bakes.com +noall +answer -t A
dig www.aiko-bakes.com +noall +answer

You should see your host's values. On Windows, use an online checker such as dns.google.

Common Mistakes

  • An old parking record left in place: the site sometimes shows an advert page instead.
  • An A record and a CNAME both on @: a CNAME blocks every other record on its host. A records on @, CNAME only on www.
  • Forgetting renewal: the site vanishes and someone else can buy the name. Keep auto-renewal on with a valid card.

Keep It Safe

  • Never share the Namecheap account. Whoever controls it controls where your name points.
  • Keep the account email current. Renewal warnings go there.
  • Lock the domain. Under Manage, Sharing & Transfer, section Transfer Out: if Domain Lock offers Lock, click it. A locked name cannot be moved to another registrar.

Key Takeaways

  • A domain is a name you rent every year from a registrar; DNS is the address book that points it at your host
  • Judge the price by the renewal, not the first year. Keep auto-renewal and free Domain Privacy on, and turn on two-factor
  • GitHub Pages: set the custom domain first, then four A records on @ and a CNAME on www, then Enforce HTTPS
  • Fly.io: fly certs add prints the records. A Tailscale Funnel (.ts.net) address cannot use your own domain
  • Delete old parking records, never mix A and CNAME on @, check with dig, and keep the domain locked
